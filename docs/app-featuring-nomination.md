## The pitch (nomination "tell us about your app" field)

Be A Number starts where no app can: on a shirt. Every Be A Number
shirt carries a number on the back, and every number is a real child
at a school campus in Omoro District, Northern Uganda. The wearer
downloads the app, enters their number, and holds the screen —
Hold to Meet — until the kid on the other end is revealed: name,
face, what they love.

From there the app is a correspondence engine with one child, not a
donation platform. You write; the campus team translates; a real
reply comes back in the kid's own handwriting, scanned into your
thread. A letter's two-week journey to Uganda is visible stage by
stage — Sent, Reviewed, Translated, In their hands. The app knows
what time it is at the campus ("It's just after sunrise in Omoro —
Ismail is getting ready for school") so the connection feels live
between letters, because it is.

There are no streaks, points, or badges anywhere in the app. The
retention mechanic is a nine-year-old writing back.

Be A Number, International is a US 501(c)(3). Sponsorship is $25 a
month; the shirt is the only door in.

## What's genuinely unique (editors' criteria mapping)

- **Innovation / uniqueness**: physical-object-to-relationship reveal
  (a bearer-instrument number on a shirt) — no roster browsing, no
  choosing a child from a grid. The match already exists; the app
  reveals it.
- **UX**: one reveal moment engineered like a ceremony (hold gesture,
  haptics, staged reveal); letter journey as ambient anticipation;
  campus-clock presence lines.
- **Design**: single warm system (cream/ink/gold, Lora + Inter),
  Instagram grammar for the feed, zero dashboard energy.
- **Social good**: real named children, community-led campus (local
  team of thirty runs the day), correspondence both directions.

## Product page requirements (before submitting)

Screenshots that SHOW the two things no other app has:
1. The reveal mid-hold (number on screen, "keep holding…").
2. The revealed kid page — face, name, presence line visible.
3. A thread with a scanned handwritten reply — the real artifact.
4. The letter journey dots mid-flight.
5. Home feed with a campus video card.

Preview video: 15–20s — shirt → number → hold → reveal → reply
arriving. The physical-to-digital arc is the whole story; film the
shirt first.

Copy rule for the listing: voice.md applies. No "empower," no
"impact" as a verb, no savior framing. The kids are having an
ordinary school year; that's the point.
