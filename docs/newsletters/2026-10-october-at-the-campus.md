Paste this into the BodyHTML field. Plain `<p>`, `<h2>`, `<ul>` only, per docs/claude/newsletter.md.

```html
<h2>Twenty-five new kids</h2>
<p>Hope Bridge closed Term II with 65 learners. When Term III opened on 14 September, 25 new kids showed up for their first day. The school is now at 90.</p>
<p>Families around Omoro District are choosing Hope Bridge, and they're telling their neighbors. A 38 percent jump in one term doesn't come from a poster. It comes from parents talking.</p>
<p>Simon's team is finishing intake for the new kids now. Once they're added to the roster, they'll start showing up behind shirt numbers in the next batch, and you'll meet them the same way you met everyone else.</p>

<h2>In the classroom</h2>
<p>Teachers are giving short assessments all term long, so a kid who's falling behind gets help in the third week instead of finding out at exams.</p>
<img src="nursery-classroom.jpg" alt="Nursery class at Hope Bridge, kids at red desks with the vowel chart on the wall">
<p>The nursery room is where most of the new faces are. Red desks, a vowel chart on the wall, and a teacher walking the rows. Teachers also say more kids are asking questions and joining class discussions they used to sit out.</p>
<img src="primary-classroom.jpg" alt="Upper primary class at Hope Bridge during a lesson">

<h2>Vaccines at the school</h2>
<img src="vaccination-outreach.jpg" alt="Community health workers vaccinating children under a tree at Hope Bridge">
<p>This quarter Hope Bridge hosted a measles and rubella vaccination for children from 8 months to 5 years old. A community health team set up under the tree by the field with a cooler of vaccine and a box of syringes, and the little ones lined up in their uniforms. Families from the surrounding villages brought their children to the school instead of making the trip to a health center. We'll post the count once YDO sends it.</p>
<p>The medical center on campus treats school kids and neighbors alike. When a kid gets sick, the staff there can deal with it before it turns into a week out of class.</p>

<h2>What's next</h2>
<p>Ninety kids is good news, and it puts real pressure on the campus. Simon's team named four things to get ready before the next school year:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Water.</strong> The school needs a reliable source for drinking, washing, cooking and, later, farming.</li>
<li><strong>Classrooms.</strong> Every kid moves up a class next year and more are expected to enroll. The rooms we have won't hold everyone.</li>
<li><strong>Staff housing.</strong> Teachers live out in the surrounding community right now. Housing on campus would keep them close and safe, and on hand when kids need them.</li>
<li><strong>Sports and music.</strong> Equipment, instruments and costumes for music, dance and drama, plus somewhere to use them.</li>
</ul>
<img src="dance-time.jpg" alt="Kids and staff dancing in a room at the campus">
<p>That last one is already happening without any of it. This is dance time in a spare room, no instruments, no costumes, no stage.</p>
<p>We don't have prices on any of these yet. When Simon's team sends estimates, we'll post them here with what each one costs.</p>
<p>Every shirt and every $25 month goes toward the campus budget that keeps all 90 kids in class. Thank you for being part of it.</p>

<h2>Christmas at the campus</h2>
<p>Christmas giving opens next month. Every kid gets the same gift, and you'll see yours with theirs. Watch this page.</p>
```
