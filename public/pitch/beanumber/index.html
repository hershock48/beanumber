A proposal, after the fact. September 2026

Every number is a child. This is the site that carries them.

beanumber.org has been live since January. It has never been written down what it is, what it runs on, or what it costs to keep. This page does that, and puts the agreement behind it in writing.

What the site is

Nine things, one codebase, 776 commits.

Most of it is invisible from the homepage. That is the point of writing it down: a board should know what it owns, and what would stop if the monthly stopped.

The mechanic

The number is printed on the shirt. The site turns it into a name.

One design, four colorways, $25. The buyer reads the number off the shirt, types it in, holds the button, and meets the child. Nobody on the admin side ever assigns a child to a buyer; the site refuses to. Numbers above 53 fold back onto the roster through a batch table, so a fourth run of shirts still lands on the same fifty-three children, and a sponsor of child 15 is not mistaken for the holder of every shirt that maps to 15.

See it: beanumber.org/shirts

Sponsor accounts, with no passwords

Sign in is an emailed link that lasts a day and a session that lasts a year. Inside: every child the sponsor knows, notes to the child with translation on the campus side, report cards and letters, the campus newsfeed and awards. Buying a shirt makes a holder; $25 a month makes a sponsor, and a child’s personal updates are for sponsors.

The door: beanumber.org/signin

Payments, on Be A Number’s own Stripe

One-time and monthly gifts, the shirt with an optional $25 a month continuation that first charges on day thirty, the sponsorship itself, gift sponsorships, the farmers-market booth checkout on a phone, repeat shirts and numbered merch for sponsors, promo codes. One webhook records every order, mints the sponsor code, enrolls the buyer in the right drip, and sends the receipt.

See it: beanumber.org/donate · /gift

Email that runs itself

Sent through the charity’s own Gmail with a fallback behind it. Six drip sequences, chosen by what the person did: bought a shirt online, bought one at the booth, became a sponsor, gave once, gave monthly. The campus newsletter has an editor, a schedule, a dry-run recipient count and one-click unsubscribe, and every issue becomes its own page for search.

The archive: beanumber.org/news

The admin

A roster where the campus enters children and Kevin approves the changes field by field. A penpal queue with translation and a print sheet. Fulfillment with a Pirate Ship import file and packing slips sorted by size. Donors, the sponsor reveal board, retention and monthly recurring revenue, Stripe sync, batches, student of the month, thermal bag labels.

The door: beanumber.org/admin

The public pages

The founder story, the 2025 impact page, partnerships, governance with the 2025 financials, the annual report and the impact and financial summary, contact, privacy and terms. And the Youth Development Organisation’s own pages under /ydo, with their own navigation, so the campus partner has a web presence without a second site to keep.

See it: /governance · /reports · /ydo

The data, and the jobs

A Postgres database of twenty-four tables with an audit log on every write, and photo and document storage beside it. Five jobs run every day and one on Sundays: overdue-update reminders, scheduled posts, the newsletter, the drips, push delivery, and the Sunday ping to sponsors before the letter batch leaves the campus.

Not a page. The schedule is in the repository’s vercel.json.

The mobile app’s back end

Twenty-four API routes for the app: sign in with Apple and Google, push notifications, claims, the child threads, the campus feed, deferred links, account deletion. Served from the same project as the site, so there is one database and one truth. The app itself waits on the developer accounts, which are the charity’s to open.

The deep-link files: /.well-known/apple-app-site-association
Also in the repository

The print files, the videos, and the numbers behind the children.

The shirt insert card, the bag labels, the booth banner and kit, the founder letters, the vinyl cut files, and the impact report and reel that render from code. None of it is a website, all of it is yours, and it travels with the repository the day you ask for it.

The story it serves: beanumber.org/founder
What is still owed to the site

Two things, both ours to do and both in the monthly. Airtable is no longer a data source, but the site still needs its keys to start and still calls it from the rep portal; that comes out. And beanumber.org is missing the two DNS records that stop mail from kevin@beanumber.org carrying an authentication warning in Gmail; that goes in the day we have the registrar login.

The part that matters most

Nothing stands between a gift and the campus.

A $25 gift on beanumber.org is charged on the charity’s own Stripe account and settles to the charity’s own bank. No platform takes a percentage on the way through, and nothing in the site is rented from anyone who could switch it off. That is not the normal way a small nonprofit’s site is built, and it is worth saying out loud once.

The accounts are the charity’s

Stripe, the Gmail the receipts send from, the domain, the app store accounts. Glazed Web holds none of them. The hosting project and the database run under the studio’s accounts today, paid for by the monthly, and transfer to accounts the charity names whenever it asks.

The records are the charity’s

Every donor, sponsor, order and email, and every child’s name, photo, number, report card and letter. The children’s records are the most sensitive thing on the site, and the agreement says what we do with them: only this work, never copied out, never in our marketing, never in search.

The site is the charity’s

Code, content, the repository, every login. There is no build fee to pay off first. If the monthly ever stopped, the site would keep running wherever the charity chose to host it, and we would help it move.

One thing the board should know rather than discover. Kevin founded Be A Number and owns Glazed Web, so he sits on both sides of this agreement. A charity paying a related party is ordinary, and it is done properly with a board vote recorded in the minutes and a signature from someone other than Kevin. The agreement page is built for that: whoever signs types their own name.

What the monthly buys

Care, not permission.

The monthly is the part a client forgets it is buying. Here it is in four pieces, so nobody has to guess in month six.

1

Hosting and keeping it standing

Every day, without being asked

The hosting, the SSL certificate, security and dependency updates as they come, backups, the database and the photo storage the site runs on, the six scheduled jobs, and the Stripe webhook kept working through every change Stripe makes. Domain renewal too, where we hold the domain.

2

Send a text, get an edit

Up to an hour a month

A price, a page of copy, a photo, a redirect, a new report published. Children, sponsors, orders, newsletters and penpal notes are not edits; the admin exists so the charity does those itself, as often as it likes, without a ticket to anyone.

3

Airtable out

Owed, and in the monthly

The boot requirement, the thousand-line library nothing imports, and the four routes that still call it. The rep portal moves onto the database with everything else, so the site depends on nothing the charity does not use.

4

Email that lands clean

Owed, and in the monthly

The two DNS records on beanumber.org that Gmail and Proton look for before they trust a sender. Ten minutes once we have the registrar login, and every receipt and newsletter afterwards arrives without a warning above it.

What is quoted separately, always in writing first. New features, new pages beyond an edit, and anything beyond the hour, at $125 an hour, approved before it starts. Nothing lands on a bill unannounced. The mobile app’s store submissions, photography and video production, and shirt printing are the charity’s own costs and are not part of this.

What it costs

Nothing to build. $50 a month to keep.

The site was built at no charge, and the agreement records that in writing so nobody ever has to wonder. The monthly is the only number.
