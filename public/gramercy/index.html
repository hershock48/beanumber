For families ($25/mo) For donors + partners

There’s a small Christian school we support in Northern Uganda. Every Be A Number shirt has a Number pressed on the back that belongs to a real student there.

Every Warrior at Gramercy gets a shirt. On the first day of school the whole class meets their kids together. From there the Warriors write once a month, the mail bag from Uganda opens once a month at assembly, and the pairing sticks as long as your kid is at Gramercy.

What the school year looks like

One kickoff, then a monthly rhythm.

One big assembly in August when everyone meets their kid. A short writing block once a month after that. A mail bag from Uganda that opens at monthly assembly. That’s the whole shape.

01
First week of school · Meeting Day

The whole school meets their kids.

Every Warrior gets their shirt at assembly, flips it, and finds a Number on the back. On the big screen they Hold to Meet, one class at a time, and their pen pal shows up. Photo, name, a couple lines about who this kid is. From that morning on they’re paired.

02
Once a month · Writing time

The classroom writes.

A short block, maybe 20 minutes, folded into Bible or homeroom. K5 through 2nd draw a picture and dictate a line or two. 3rd through 6th write a paragraph. Upper school writes a real letter with a prayer request. Kara gets the prompt packet from us so no teacher has to invent the lesson.

03
Once a month · Letters Day

The mail bag from Uganda opens.

Same monthly assembly. Some letters read aloud, the rest sent home in backpacks. Some months bring more than others (Uganda has holidays and rainy seasons too), but every reply is in the kid’s own hand.

For Kara and the teachers

Nobody at Gramercy has to build this from scratch.

We hand the school everything you’d need on your end to actually run the program. It maps to the ACSI Great Commission Project framework Gramercy already teaches, so no teacher has to invent a new curriculum. It’s the working version of a missions unit that’s already in your Bible class.

  • ◆ Teacher packet PDF. Six lessons, grade-differentiated K5–12. Print or project. Ready for the first day of school.
  • ◆ Assembly slide decks. Meeting Day for the first week, plus one for each monthly Letters Day. Chapel-ready. My intro video included.
  • ◆ Prompt library. 20+ age-graded writing prompts by month, plus prayer requests and drawing prompts for lower school.
  • ◆ Prayer cards + hall poster. Each student takes home a printed card with their pen pal’s photo, name, and one prayer request. Big Uganda map for the hallway.
  • ◆ Monthly campus video. Two-minute update from the Uganda campus. Something Kara or a teacher can play at chapel or in Bible class.
  • ◆ Cross-curricular hooks. Suggested ties into geography (find Uganda), math ($25 × 12 × 350), Bible (the Great Commission is not abstract now), art (illustrate your letter).
  • ◆ Optional annual video call. One live 20-minute call from the Uganda campus to a Gramercy assembly, once per school year. Our team on the ground hosts.
ACSI alignment

Fits the framework Gramercy already teaches.

ACSI’s Great Commission Project asks Christian schools to partner long-term with a specific place and a specific group of people. That’s what this is. A real campus with real kids that your school stays connected to year after year.

It also fits the K–12 progression ACSI already teaches: little kids pray for a place, middle grades write letters, upper school funds and eventually travels. This program lets Gramercy do all three at once, with the same kids on the other end.

How the letters actually get across

I want to be straight about how this works.

Every other sponsorship program promises letters. Most of them never deliver. Here’s exactly what happens on our end so nobody at Gramercy is surprised.

01
At Gramercy

Your Warrior writes at school. Paper or Chromebook, up to the teacher.

›
02
At the campus

Our team in Uganda reads the letter to your Warrior’s pen pal and helps them write a reply by hand.

›
03
Back to Gramercy

The handwritten reply comes back with the mail bag. Your Warrior sees the actual letter.

I’m not going to promise every kid gets a reply every single month. Uganda has its own weeks, its own holidays, its own rainy seasons. What I’ll promise is that every letter your Warrior sends actually gets read to their pen pal, and every reply that comes back is that kid’s own words in their own hand.

Two ways to pay for it

Some families will. Others will need a donor.

Some Gramercy families will cover their own kid. That’s the classic path. But this only works if every Warrior gets in, and we know not every family can swing $25 a month. So a Rotary chapter, a foundation, or a Warrior Club donor can underwrite a class, a grade, or the whole school. Nobody at Gramercy gets left out.

This has been done before

Legacy Christian Academy up in Minnesota has been doing something like this since 2007. They started with 100 kids at a Uganda campus and now they’re at 300. World Vision runs a version of it in schools across 40 states through their Ignite program. Christian school plus East African campus is a real, working model.

Gramercy would be the first Be A Number partner school anywhere.
